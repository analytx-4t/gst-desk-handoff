# Four high-severity faults block this GSTR-9/9C builder from filing

**Verdict: the tool is a sound reconciliation worksheet for GSTR-9 and 9C (FY 2025-26, due 31 Dec 2026), but it is not yet safe to use as a source for keying the return.** Its totals logic (Tables 4, 5, 6O, 7I/7J, 9C Tables 5, 7 and 12) matches the form structure the notes could reach, and its 9C Table 5 signs match the printed form. Its row labelling in GSTR-9 Table 7 is wrong against the notified form, and several rows marked "input" have no field to type into. Three more problems would produce wrong figures in Table 4 and Table 5: it ignores the reverse-charge and SEZ flags on B2B invoices, it books every export in 4C (so 5A is always nil), and it drops B2C credit notes. Your working assumption for Table 7 (7A rule 37, 7A1 rule 37A, 7A2 rule 38, 7B rule 39, 7C rule 42, 7D rule 43, 7E section 17(5), 7F TRAN-I, 7G TRAN-II, 7H other) is confirmed by the amended form text, and the tool's earlier labels (7B-7F, 7G-7H) do not match it. No FY 2025-26 table change was found in any source: the portal enabled the forms on 8 Oct 2026 and the only stated condition is that all FY 2025-26 GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B are filed ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/gst/gst-portal-enables-gstr-99c-annual-returns-for-fy-2025-26), secondary-source). So everything here rests on the FY 2024-25 rule set carried forward by inference.

**What could not be read.** No ICAI row-level text was read: the ICAI Dec 2025 technical guide returned only Part I and the 9C Table 5A to 5F passage, and its Tables 7, 9 and 12 text and its reconciling-item chapters were not retrievable, so every ICAI statement below comes from press summaries (secondary-source). No official consolidated post-amendment GSTR-9C form could be read; the 9C structure was assembled from a Chhattisgarh GST copy of the form dated before the 2025 amendment plus the text of Notification 13/2025-CT, which I (the research team) stitched together. The GSTR-9 form text came from a state-site mirror ([cggst.com](https://www.cggst.com/public/uploads/document/1761124402-r-09.pdf)) of the FY 2024-25 version, not from the CBIC or GSTN original. Every document tagged primary-source was read through a summarising fetch tool, not the raw PDF. No offline utility was reviewed, and the notes found no mention of an FY 2025-26 utility release.

**Labels used.** The labels follow the research notes and are not upgraded. *Primary-source* means a GSTN FAQ, a CBIC notification or circular, or the form itself, read through a summariser. *Primary-source (mirror)* means the form or notification text read on a third-party or state-site copy. *Secondary-source* means TaxGuru, StudyCafe, Taxscan, HNA LLP, BCAS and similar. *Unverified* means a single weak source or background knowledge in the notes. *Code* means my own reading of `engine/analyze.js`, `engine/parsers.js` and `ui/app.js`, not a claim from a source.

## Table-by-table comparison against the sources

### GSTR-9 Tables 4 and 5

The form allows no netting in 4B to 4E for FY 2024-25: the net option exists only for FY 2017-18 to 2020-21, so credit notes go to 4I, debit notes to 4J and amendments to 4K/4L, while 4A is net and 4G is net of credit and debit notes (primary-source (mirror), [cggst form](https://www.cggst.com/public/uploads/document/1761124402-r-09.pdf)). The tool agrees on principle and its own message says "4B is shown gross", but it is gross only for months where the full GSTR-1 JSON exists. In other months it derives 4B as the HSN net total less B2C and exports, which nets credit notes into 4B and also leaves exempt, SEZ and deemed-export values inside it (Code; whether the HSN table includes exempt supplies is background knowledge, unverified). One TaxGuru article says netting in 4B to 4E is still allowed; it conflicts with the form text and should be ignored ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/common-errors-correct-reporting-taxable-sales-gstr-9-24-25.html), secondary-source).

Table 5 is where the tool is weakest on source fidelity. 5D, 5E and 5F come from GSTR-1 Table 8 and are shown separately, which is always allowed: 5F must be separate, and exempt and nil-rated may be combined in the "exempted" row for the listed years (primary-source (mirror), [cggst form](https://www.cggst.com/public/uploads/document/1761124402-r-09.pdf)); whether that combining option runs to FY 2025-26 is unverified. 5A, 5B and 5C are fixed at zero, which is wrong whenever LUT exports, SEZ supplies without tax or RCM outward supplies exist. 5H to 5K (credit notes, debit notes, amendments) are zero with no input, and the sources disagree on whether they may be reported net for FY 2024-25: the Notification 13/2025-CT summary and two secondary articles say yes, while the form mirror lists 5I to 5K only to FY 2021-22 (conflict, noted in the notes as "lean allowed"). The tool's 5N is 4N plus 5M less 4G, where its "5M" is only the 5G subtotal. The notes could not verify how 5N treats RCM inward supplies, and the sources disagree on whether it includes s.9(5) supplies, so this formula is unverified and should be checked on the utility.

### GSTR-9 Table 6

The tool reads 6A from 3B 4A(1) to (5), 6C-6D from 4A(3), 6E from 4A(1), 6G from 4A(4), all matching the notes (primary-source, [GSTN FAQ](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/downloads/news/faq_on_gstr9_for_24_25_dt_15_oct_25_v6_final.pdf) and form mirror). Its 6F uses 4A(2); the notes' table lists the 6F source as "4(A)(1) (expected)" and flags the 6F instruction text as not captured (unverified), so 6F needs checking against the utility. The form defines 6A1 as preceding-year ITC availed in the April to October 3B filed by 30 November, 6A2 as 6A less 6A1, and 6J as the auto-computed difference between 6A2 and 6B to 6H, "ideally zero" (primary-source, GSTN FAQ and Notification 13/2025). The tool's 6A1 is a 2B-date proxy and its 6J is forced to nil (see fix 5). For FY 2025-26 the 6A1 window is by analogy April to October 2026 (unverified). The tool's 6H label ("rule 37/37A reclaims, in the year reclaimed") is narrower than the FAQ. Under FAQ 7 a same-year reversal for another reason, such as goods not received, is claimed in 6B, reversed in 7H and reclaimed in 6H, and a prior-year other-reason reclaim goes to 6A1 (primary-source, GSTN FAQ). Treating all of 3B 4D(1) as 6H therefore misplaces the prior-year non-37/37A reclaims. That 6A already includes the reclaim is the notes' inference from FAQ 7 (the claim-reverse-reclaim appears twice in 6A); no source names 4D(1) (unverified).

The capital-goods split in 6B is mandatory and the tool produces it from register flags, while the inputs/input-services split is optional and the tool combines them (primary-source (mirror)). The tool shows 6E as one row, whereas the notes treat a 6E inputs/capital-goods split as probably required for FY 2024-25 (unverified; TaxGuru says the combined option was withdrawn, [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/relaxations-optional-tables-gstr-9-gstr-9c-fy-2024-25.html), secondary-source). 6K to 6N are zero with no input.

### GSTR-9 Table 7

This is the table with the clearest conflict between tool and source. The amended form labels, in order, are: 7A as per Rule 37, 7A1 as per Rule 37A, 7A2 as per rule 38, 7B Rule 39, 7C Rule 42, 7D Rule 43, 7E section 17(5), 7F reversal of TRAN-I credit, 7G reversal of TRAN-II credit, 7H other reversals, 7I total, 7J net ITC available (6O less 7I) (primary-source (mirror), [cggst form](https://www.cggst.com/public/uploads/document/1761124402-r-09.pdf)). The notification text lists 7A1 and 7A2 in its headings ([Notification 13/2025-CT](https://gstlearn.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/centaltax-13-2025.pdf), primary-source (mirror)), and the HNA article that swaps 7A1 and 7A2 is internally inconsistent (secondary-source). The mapping you proposed is therefore correct. Section 18(4) has no dedicated row, so it falls into 7H, the residual for ITC-03 reversals and "any other reversal not specified in any row above" (inference in the notes, unverified as a specific placement). Some commentary uses "7H1" for the last row, but the form mirror and the notification text show only 7H (unverified for a 7H1 row).

Two further rules affect the tool's Table 7. The option to club 7A to 7E into 7H ran only to FY 2023-24 on the notification text, so it is unavailable for FY 2024-25 and, by extension, FY 2025-26 (TaxGuru agrees; StudyCafe says otherwise, so the conflict is noted: primary-source (mirror) over secondary-source). Table 7 covers current-year ITC only, so reversals of prior-year ITC in the current 3B do not belong in it and 7J can legitimately differ from 3B 4C (secondary-source, [A2Z on GSTN additional FAQs of 4 Dec 2025](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/gstn-issues-additional-important-faqs-on-gstr-9-gstr-9c-to-clarify-reporting-of-itc-rcm-and-reconciliation-requirements/); the original GSTN document was not fetched). The tool's 7I takes all 3B 4B reversals, which overstates 7I for any prior-year reversal; the tool itself says it cannot tell them apart. The form also ties 7E to 3B 4D: no 7E entry unless the 4D amount was included in 4A (primary-source (mirror)), a dependency the tool does not model.

### GSTR-9 Table 8, Table 9, Table 17 and the unbuilt tables

For Table 8 the portal's 8A is built from GSTR-2B for the FY plus FY invoices appearing in the April to October 2B of the next year, excludes earlier-year invoices appearing in the April to October 2B of the current year, and the online 8A prevails over the invoice-wise Excel download (GSTN FAQ summaries: [vjmglobal](https://www.vjmglobal.com/blog/gstn-issued-faqs-on-newly-released-form-gstr-9-9c-for-fy-2024-25), [A2Z](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/gstn-issued-very-important-list-of-frequently-asked-questions-faqs-on-gstr-9-9c-for-the-financial-year-2024-25/), secondary-source; the GSTN FAQ itself is primary-source but was read through summaries). The tool's 8A(a) approximates the first part and its 8A(b) is a manual input, which is a reasonable design; no source uses an "8A(b)" label. One author reads portal 8A as including blocked-credit documents and excluding imports, RCM, ISD and POS-restricted ITC ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gstr-9-table-8a-8b-key-fy-2024-25-changes.html), secondary-source, author's reading), whereas the tool counts eligible rows only. 8B is correctly drawn from 6B only, with 6H excluded, from FY 2024-25 (primary-source, GSTN FAQ 18). 8C as ITC of the year first claimed next year within the specified period, and not reclaimed-after-reversal ITC, is consistent with the tool's note (primary-source, GSTN FAQ 16). The tool's 8C input is "3B 4A(5) of the next year", which is a total and not invoice-linked; 8C of one year should equal 6A1 of the next (a control check from [HNA LLP](https://hnallp.com/assets/articles/7ccaa-changes-compliances-in-gstr-9-9c_oct-25_f-1.pdf), secondary-source). 8E to 8K, including 8G, 8H1 and 8I, are not built; the notes did not confirm the 8E to 8K labels against the form.

Table 9 on the portal takes the positive net liability from 3B Table 6.1, leaves the field blank if the net is negative, keeps it editable, and has non-editable paid columns (primary-source, [GSTN FAQ](https://tutorial.gst.gov.in/downloads/news/faq_on_gstr9_for_24_25_dt_15_oct_25_v6_final.pdf), FAQ 20). From FY 2024-25 it shows total tax paid (cash plus ITC) and a difference column, with positive variances needing reasons and negative variances proof of payment (secondary-source, [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gstr-9-gstr-9c-fy-2024-25-major-key-points.html), [HNA LLP](https://hnallp.com/assets/articles/7ccaa-changes-compliances-in-gstr-9-9c_oct-25_f-1.pdf)). The sources disagree on whether a positive difference is paid by DRC-03 or DRC-07 (conflict, unresolved), and HNA says it can now be paid using ITC. The tool's Table 9 gives payable, cash and ITC by head and interest and late fee from 3B 5.1, but not the difference column.

Table 17 is mandatory from FY 2021-22, six-digit HSN above Rs 5 crore and four-digit for B2B only up to Rs 5 crore (secondary-source, [HNA LLP](https://hnallp.com/assets/articles/7ccaa-changes-compliances-in-gstr-9-9c_oct-25_f-1.pdf), [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/is-hsn-wise-summary-mandatory-in-gstr-9)). One source (Taxroutine, [link](https://taxroutine.com/changes-in-gstr-9/)) adds an optional position up to Rs 1.50 crore with no B2B supplies; it is single-source and unverified. The tool aggregates GSTR-1 HSN rows by HSN, rate and UQC and flags short HSN against 6 or 4 digits on its 5N turnover. Which year's turnover governs the digit test in Table 17 was not established (unverified), and the 4-digit rule is for B2B only while the tool applies it to all lines. The portal offers a GSTR-1/1A Table 12 HSN Excel in Table 17 layout (secondary-source, A2Z and vjmglobal above), a ready cross-check for the tool's output. The 65% concessional-rate checkbox was removed from Tables 17 and 18 for FY 2024-25 (secondary-source, A2Z).

Tables 10 to 16 and 18 are not built, which the tool states. Tables 12 and 13 are described as compulsory where such ITC exists (secondary-source, [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gstr-9-gstr-9c-fy-2024-25-major-key-points.html)), but StudyCafe says Tables 10 to 14 are mandatory while HNA lists 12 and 13 as relaxations; the status of Tables 10, 11 and 14 is unresolved. Tables 15, 16 and 18 are optional on one secondary-source ([HNA LLP](https://hnallp.com/assets/articles/7ccaa-changes-compliances-in-gstr-9-9c_oct-25_f-1.pdf)). Because Table 13 must hold FY 2025-26 ITC claimed in the April to November 2026 3B, and Table 12 the ITC reversed in the next year, the tool's "not built" status leaves a mandatory-looking gap for many filers.

### GSTR-9C bridge: Tables 5, 7, 9 and 12

The tool's 9C Table 5 matches the printed row IDs and signs: 5A base, plus for 5B, 5C, 5D, 5F and 5J, minus for 5E, 5G, 5H, 5I, 5K and 5L, plus/minus for 5M, 5N and 5O, 5R equal to 5Q less 5P (primary-source (mirror), [Chhattisgarh form copy](https://www.cggst.com/uploads/document/1748519936-r-9c.pdf), which predates the 2025 amendment; BCAS labels agree, [BCAS](https://bcasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/GST-Annual-Return-GSTR-9-and-Reconciliation-Statement-GSTR-9C.pdf), secondary-source). The wording of 5G to 5L in the notes was lightly paraphrased by summarisers, so ids and signs are firmer than labels. Whether 5B to 5N must be shown separately is disputed: HNA and one TaxGuru article say 5C to 5N must be separate and only 5B may be merged into 5O, while TaxGuru's relaxations article, StudyCafe and TaxGst say 5B to 5N are optional (conflict, secondary-source on both sides). The tool shows all rows, which is the safe course. It hard-codes 5G and 5L to zero ("not applicable"), which is wrong for a composition switcher in 5L. Its 5Q adds "previous year additions" for GSTR-9 Tables 10 and 11; the notes carry no source for that addition to 5Q (unverified).

In Table 7 the tool has 7A to 7G and the correct 7G = 7F less 7E, but misses 7D1 and the amended 7E formula, and builds 7F as 4N less 4G, a formula the notes do not confirm (unverified). In Table 9 the tool has only 9P, 9Q and 9R in aggregate, with no rate-wise rows (the form's rows run from the 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%, 3%, 0.25%, 0.10% rows to reverse-charge rows, interest, late fee, penalty and others), and no 9K2 row for e-commerce operators. Notification 13/2025-CT changed 9Q from "paid" to "payable", which the tool already follows, and the tool's rate-wise support table rounds to the nearest slab, which the tool itself warns can create small differences (primary-source (mirror), [Notification 13/2025-CT](https://cdn.taxo.online/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/centaltax-13-2025.pdf)). Table 12 in the form ends at 12F; the notes found no 12G in any copy, so the tool is right not to have one. 12E is net ITC in GSTR-9 Table 7J (primary-source (mirror)), and GSTN has said 7J holds current-year net ITC only, so 12B (earlier-year ITC claimed now) and reversals of earlier-year ITC create expected differences explained in Table 13 (secondary-source, [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/news?id=63688), [TaxO](https://taxo.online/latest-news/05-12-2025-gstn-has-issued-faqs-on-gstr-9-9c-for-fy-2024-25/)). The tool computes 12D as 12A plus 12B less 12C, which is the arithmetic implied by the printed signs but not a printed formula (unverified). Tables 11, 13, 14, 15, 16 and Part V, and the new Table 17 (late fee payable and paid, with rows for integrated, central and State/UT tax), are not in the tool. Table 14 is optional on all sources.

## How the rules changed from FY 2021-22 to FY 2025-26

The big structural change is a single notification. Notification 13/2025-CT dated 17 Sep 2025 (the CGST Third Amendment Rules, effective 22 Sep 2025) rewrote both forms from FY 2024-25: new rows 6A1, 6A2, 7A1, 7A2, 8H1, 6H redefined to reclaimed ITC only, 6J auto-populated, 8B delinked from 6H, Table 9 split into payable and paid, Tables 12 and 13 relabelled, GSTR-1A data feeding Tables 4 and 5, and in 9C the new 7D1, 9K2, "cash or ITC" in Table 11 and Part V, "paid" changed to "payable" in 9Q, and Table 17 (primary-source (mirror), [Notification 13/2025-CT](https://cdn.taxo.online/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/centaltax-13-2025.pdf) and [form mirror](https://www.cggst.com/public/uploads/document/1761124402-r-09.pdf); outline confirmed by [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/09/18/central-goods-and-services-tax-third-amendment-2025-legal-compliance-update/), secondary-source). The notes found no FY 2025-26 change. The one claimed 2026 amendment, "Notification 06/2026-CT of 10 Feb 2026", is single-sourced to a page whose dates are inconsistent and whose late-fee text was garbled, and no other source corroborates it; do not rely on it ([VirtualAuditor](https://virtualauditor.in/learn/gst-amendments-2026-latest/), unverified).

Two timing quirks recur in the sources. The enablement date and the advisory date differ (A2Z reported on 10 Oct and CAclubindia on 12 Oct, before the 13 Oct advisory date), so the portal notice probably preceded the formal advisory (inference). And the "specified period" is stated as April to October 3B in some sources and "up to 30 November" in others; the GSTN FAQ resolves it as the April to October GSTR-3B filed by 30 November ("specified time period is 30 November 2025" for FY 2024-25), so the two statements are the same window (primary-source, GSTN FAQ 8). For FY 2025-26 the equivalent is April to October 2026 filed by 30 November 2026 (unverified, by analogy). Nothing in the notes explains how the 22 Sep 2025 GST rate changes affect any row in Tables 4 to 7; rate-wise detail sits in Tables 9, 17 and 18 and the year now straddles two rate regimes ([NBA Office](https://www.nbaoffice.com/gstr-9-gstr-9c-fy-2025-26/), secondary-source).

## Applicability, due date, late fee and notices

GSTR-9 is not required where aggregate turnover is up to Rs 2 crore, under Notification 15/2025-CT dated 17 Sep 2025, which applies "from the financial year 2024-25 onwards" and so covers FY 2025-26 without a new notification (the text was read from a mirror: [Studycafe](https://studycafe.in/govt-exempts-small-businesses-from-gst-annual-return-filing-read-notification-393480.html), [Lexplosion](https://lexplosion.in/cbic-exempts-taxpayers-with-aggregate-turnover-upto-rs-2-crores-from-filing-gst-annual-return-for-financial-year-2024-25-onwards/), secondary-source; the notes elsewhere tag the notification text as primary-source (mirror)). Whether the exemption is "up to" or "less than" Rs 2 crore differs in one source ([Studycafe](https://studycafe.in/cbic-issues-4-notifications-to-notify-changes-in-rules-related-to-gstr-9-and-gstr-9c-gst-refund-and-more-393770.html)); most say "up to", which is what the tool assumes. Between Rs 2 crore and Rs 5 crore GSTR-9 is mandatory and 9C is not; above Rs 5 crore both apply and 9C is self-certified (secondary-source, [SKM Associates](https://www.skmassociates.com/post/gstr-9-annual-return-for-fy-2025-26-due-date-exemptions-compliance-checklist), [Oquilia](https://www.oquilia.com/news/gstr-9-9c-annual-return-31-december-watchlist)). The Rs 5 crore 9C threshold from 1 Aug 2021 is in Circular 246/03/2025-GST, dated 30 Jan 2025, which also says the annual return is complete only when 9 and, if required, 9C are filed ([CBIC circular](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/cir-cgst-246-03-2025.pdf), primary-source). Aggregate turnover is PAN-wide and all-India, including exempt supplies and exports and excluding RCM inward supplies (unverified, background knowledge in the notes); ICAI-guide commentary says each GST registration under a PAN that crosses the threshold is covered (secondary-source, [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=111856)). The tool tests 5N for one GSTIN, so for a multi-GSTIN PAN the answer has to be confirmed by hand. Composition taxpayers, casual taxable persons, ISDs, NRTPs and TCS collectors are described as outside GSTR-9 (secondary-source, [SKM Associates](https://www.skmassociates.com/post/gstr-9-annual-return-for-fy-2025-26-due-date-exemptions-compliance-checklist)); the statutory text was not read. Foreign airlines and OIDAR suppliers to unregistered persons are exempt from 9C by Notifications 09/2020-CT and 30/2019-CT ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-reconciliation-statement-gstr-9c), secondary-source).

The due date is 31 Dec 2026, shown on the portal, with no extension announced up to 12 to 14 Oct 2026 ([CAclubindia](https://www.caclubindia.com/news/gst-portal-enables-gstr-99c-annual-returns-for-fy-2025-26-26861.asp), [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/gst/gst-portal-enables-gstr-99c-annual-returns-for-fy-2025-26), secondary-source; no primary CBIC page was checked, and absence of news is not proof). The only recent extension found was FY 2020-21, moved from 31 Dec 2021 to 28 Feb 2022 by Notification 40/2021-CT ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/cbic-extended-due-date-of-gstr-9-and-gstr-9c-for-fy-2020-21-to-february-28th-2022/), secondary-source). For FY 2024-25, practitioner bodies asked for an extension and none had been announced by mid-December 2025; the outcome after 26 Dec 2025 was not found ([Taxonation](https://taxonation.com/show-detail-news/2879184/gstr-9-gstr-9c-filing-tax-bodies-demand-extension-of-december-31-deadline), [CAclubindia](https://www.caclubindia.com/articles/extended-final-date-to-file-gstr-9-for-fy-202425-54497.asp), secondary-source). One source says the portal may block GSTR-3B from January 2027 if GSTR-9 is not filed; treat it as speculation ([NBA Office](https://www.nbaoffice.com/gstr-9-gstr-9c-fy-2025-26/), weak secondary-source). A reported portal glitch (the tile demanding returns already filed) rests on one source ([A2Z](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/gstr-9-gstr-9c-filing-enabled-for-fy-2025-26-on-gst-portal/), unverified). ITC for FY 2025-26 can be availed up to 30 Nov 2026 or the annual return date, whichever is earlier (secondary-source, A2Z and NBA Office), and the portal's 8A freezes around 1 Dec, so for FY 2024-25 one firm advised filing after that date ([HNA LLP](https://hnallp.com/assets/articles/7ccaa-changes-compliances-in-gstr-9-9c_oct-25_f-1.pdf), secondary-source). The three-year bar on filing returns applies, with portal enforcement dates that conflict between 1 Aug and 1 Oct 2025 in two sources ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gstn-advisory-file-pending-gst-returns-expiry-years.html), [Taxo](https://taxo.online/latest-news/10-09-2025-gstn-issued-advisory-to-file-pending-returns-before-expiry-of-three-years-effective-from-01-10-2025/), secondary-source). Neither form can be revised once filed (secondary-source for 9C, [HNA LLP](https://hnallp.com/assets/articles/7ccaa-changes-compliances-in-gstr-9-9c_oct-25_f-1.pdf); unverified for GSTR-9).

Late fee under s.47(2) is charged once for the whole annual return, from the due date to the date the complete return (the later of GSTR-9 and 9C) is filed (primary-source, [Circular 246/03/2025-GST](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/cir-cgst-246-03-2025.pdf)). The statutory rate is Rs 100 a day under each Act (Rs 200 total) capped at 0.25% under each Act (0.5% total) of State/UT turnover. Notification 07/2023-CT lowers it from FY 2022-23: for turnover up to Rs 5 crore, Rs 50 a day (25 plus 25) with a cap of 0.04% in total; for Rs 5 to 20 crore, Rs 100 a day (50 plus 50), also capped at 0.04% in total; above Rs 20 crore the statutory rate remains, which is an inference because the sources did not address that band ([Taxmann](https://taxmann.com/post/blog/cbic-reduces-late-fees-for-gst-annual-returns-notification), [GST Council PDF](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-05/07_eng.pdf), secondary-source with a primary-source summary that gave the CGST-half figures). BCAS quotes Rs 50 a day or 0.02% for up to Rs 5 crore, which conflicts with the 0.04% total on the cap for that band; since a 9C filer is above Rs 5 crore, the Rs 100 a day and 0.04% band is the one that matters and the sources agree on it, but the notification table should be read before any figure is quoted to a client ([BCAS](https://bcasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/GST-Annual-Return-GSTR-9-and-Reconciliation-Statement-GSTR-9C.pdf), secondary-source). Several outlets quote Rs 200 a day and 0.5% without the 07/2023 tiers, which is right only above Rs 20 crore. As an example under these figures, a Rs 2 crore State turnover caps the fee at Rs 8,000 (inference). For 9C the days run from the later of the due date or the GSTR-9 filing date ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gstr-9-gstr-9c-fy-2024-25-major-key-points.html), secondary-source). The Table 17 in 9C records late fee payable and paid; which tax head the portal uses for it in practice is unknown. The fee waiver for FY 2017-18 to 2022-23 under Notification 08/2025-CT (9C filed by 31 Mar 2025, no refund) does not help FY 2025-26 ([Circular 246](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/cir-cgst-246-03-2025.pdf), primary-source). A general penalty of up to Rs 25,000 per Act under s.125 appears in a 2018 article and its use for a missed 9C is background knowledge (unverified).

On notices, the one official scrutiny document the notes could open is CBIC Instruction 02/2022-GST of 22 Mar 2022 ([instruction PDF](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/instruction-no-022022-gst-dated-22032022.pdf), primary-source). It sets indicative parameters on 3B against GSTR-1 and 2A, RCM, ISD, imports, 16(4) timing, rule 42 and 43 reversals, interest and late fee, with ASMT-10 as the notice, ASMT-11 as the 30-day reply and ASMT-12 as the closure. It does not mention GSTR-9 or 9C. GSTR-9 specific triggers (GSTR-1 tax above 3B, 3B ITC above 2B, late ITC in 8C and 13, blocked credit not reversed in Table 7, RCM ITC above RCM tax, import ITC against customs, turnover below e-invoice or e-way bill data, and HSN inconsistencies) come from a software vendor's article ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/what-gstr-9-mismatches-trigger-scrutiny-notices-and-how-to-avoid-them/), secondary-source). The notes found no numeric tolerance from ICAI, GSTN or CBIC, only an explain-every-difference practice, so the tool's Rs 10 default is a firm-level setting and not a published parameter.

## Conclusion

The tool's core arithmetic is right, and several of its design choices (4B gross with a stated warning, 8B from 6B only, 9Q on "payable", no 12G, all 9C Table 5 rows shown) match the form better than commentary does. Its weaknesses are mostly in what it hides: rows marked "input" that cannot be typed into, a 6J that is nil by construction, and Table 7 labels that read as authoritative but are not. These are fixable in the code, but because the 3B-to-Table-7 mapping, the 6F source and the sign of 9R and 12F are not established by any source the notes reached, the utility has to be the last word on those.

The wider lesson is that FY 2025-26 is being filed on an FY 2024-25 rule set with no FY 2025-26 FAQ. The February-2026 amendment claim is uncorroborated, the notified form could not be read in its original, and ICAI's guide could not be read at row level. Treat the tool's output as a reconciliation pack to compare with the portal's auto-populated tables after 1 Dec 2026, not as the figures to enter.
